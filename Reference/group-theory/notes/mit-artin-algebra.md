## What this material connects

Artin’s course links abstract algebra and geometry. The reviewed handout asks which discrete plane-symmetry groups can have a two-dimensional translation lattice and point group containing only identity and one reflection. [1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-701-algebra-i-fall-2010/) [2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-701-algebra-i-fall-2010/eb0059c9e8aff119199f20080f570add_MIT18_701F10_d1group10.pdf)

## Main result of the handout

There are three group types under this classification. The permitted lattice has a rectangular form or a centered variant; translation and reflection data determine which types remain distinct. [2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-701-algebra-i-fall-2010/eb0059c9e8aff119199f20080f570add_MIT18_701F10_d1group10.pdf)

## Proof architecture

Reflection sends lattice vectors to lattice vectors. Adding and subtracting reflected vectors produces horizontal and vertical constraints. Then a nontranslation motion is written as a translation followed by reflection; squaring it constrains its glide component. [2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-701-algebra-i-fall-2010/eb0059c9e8aff119199f20080f570add_MIT18_701F10_d1group10.pdf)

## Why the last step matters

Two lattice possibilities and two motion possibilities initially suggest four cases. A coordinate adjustment identifies the centered-lattice glide case with a reflection case, leaving three types. [2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-701-algebra-i-fall-2010/eb0059c9e8aff119199f20080f570add_MIT18_701F10_d1group10.pdf)

## How to connect it to shape experiments

Distinguish a finite point group from the whole periodic symmetry group. The translation lattice carries information that a list of rotations and reflections alone cannot express. [2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-701-algebra-i-fall-2010/eb0059c9e8aff119199f20080f570add_MIT18_701F10_d1group10.pdf)

## Key terms

- **Lattice**: Integer combinations of independent translation vectors.
- **Glide reflection**: A reflection combined with translation parallel to its axis.
- **Point group**: The linear parts of the space-symmetry operations.
