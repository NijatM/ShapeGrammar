## What this text studies

Representation theory studies algebraic structures through their linear actions on vector spaces. The selected pages introduce modules and invariant subspaces, then specialize to finite groups. [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0827v5) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0827v5)

## Main result in this reading

For finite groups over complex numbers, representations decompose into irreducible pieces. Characters record traces and distinguish these decompositions; the regular representation yields the sum-of-squared-dimensions formula. [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0827v5)

## Proof and example

Maschke’s proof averages an arbitrary projector to obtain one compatible with the group action. For S3, three conjugacy classes lead to irreducible dimensions 1, 1 and 2, with 1²+1²+2²=6. [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0827v5)

## Why it matters for moonshine

A dimension gives one number; traces of group elements carry finer information. This distinction prepares the reader for graded character series rather than isolated numerical coincidences. [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0827v5)

## Key terms

- **Irreducible**: A nonzero representation with no proper nonzero invariant subspace.
- **Character**: The function assigning each group element its representation trace.
