## What this survey contributes

A historical map of moonshine connecting finite groups, modular functions, vertex algebras and mathematical physics. It prioritizes breadth and references over complete derivations. [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402345v2)

## The main conceptual distinction

A graded dimension records sizes; a graded character records how group elements act. The latter produces McKay–Thompson series and carries the stronger genus-zero claim. [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402345v2)

## How the explanation is organized

The survey moves from numerical clues and precise conjectures to the Moonshine module, the associated Lie algebra, denominator identities and modular equations. [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402345v2) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402345v2)

## What the proof discussion clarifies

Recurrence and modular-equation arguments explain how algebraic structure constrains entire series. The survey also distinguishes proving the identity from finding a satisfying conceptual explanation. [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402345v2)

## A concrete correction while reading

Equation 2.1 prints 2^45 in the Monster order. ATLAS gives 2^46; use that corrected factorization. The discrepancy is visible in the PDF and is not merely text-extraction loss. [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402345v2) [3](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/spor/M/)

## How to use it

Use the section references to identify prerequisite subjects and primary proofs. Do not treat its historical conjecture statements as a current open-problem list. [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402345v2) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402345v2)

## Key terms

- **Graded character**: Character information collected over the graded pieces.
- **Modular equation**: An algebraic relation connecting transformed function values.
