## In brief

The collection examines how data-driven drawing, modeling and simulation relate to artistic and architectural design.

Evidence: [Issue #15, Autumn 2014: opening description](https://journals.open.tudelft.nl/footprint/issue/view/394).

## Organizing question

What happens when computational representations participate in conceptual design, physical production and the subsequent operation of an artifact or building?

Evidence: [Issue #15, Autumn 2014: opening description](https://journals.open.tudelft.nl/footprint/issue/view/394).

## Editorial premise

The introduction emphasizes convergence between computational and material systems, including several possible outcomes from one representation and feedback from users or environments.

Evidence: [Issue #15, Autumn 2014: opening description](https://journals.open.tudelft.nl/footprint/issue/view/394).

## How it is organized

Bier and Knight’s opening editorial introduces five contributions from media studies, art and architecture. Their theoretical discussions and practical experiments remain separate arguments.

Evidence: [Contents: opening editorial and five contributed papers](https://journals.open.tudelft.nl/footprint/issue/view/394).

## Reading routes

Vehlken examines computational swarming; Moura and Pereira discuss collective robotics in art; Mennan addresses formalism and intuition; Hensel and Sørensen connect data-driven design with local performance-oriented architecture.

Evidence: [Contents: opening editorial and five contributed papers](https://journals.open.tudelft.nl/footprint/issue/view/394).

## What this framing establishes

The editors propose a feedback loop linking design, production and operation. The collection should be used to compare approaches, rather than treated as one test validating that loop universally.

Evidence: [Issue #15, Autumn 2014: opening description](https://journals.open.tudelft.nl/footprint/issue/view/394).
