# Affective Sleeve: Wearable Materials with Haptic Action for Promoting Calmness

## Material inspected

The full public Springer abstract was read.

Review level: **abstract-only**. The complete paper, participant counts, statistical tests and measurement protocol were not inspected. Later sleeve studies were not substituted for this 2019 study.

## What it is about

The authors develop a wearable sleeve that creates rhythmic warmth and slight pressure along the arm. They study whether its haptic pace relates to breathing and perceived calmness.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-21935-2_23)

## Premise

Material behaviour may provide affective feedback: a wearable’s physical action could influence bodily rhythm and experience.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-21935-2_23)

## Method reported

A controlled pilot compares haptic pacing with relaxed breathing pace and a pace 25% faster. Quantitative breathing observations are considered alongside qualitative self-reports.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-21935-2_23)

## Reported outcome

The abstract associates faster haptic action with faster breathing. Self-reports relate slower action to greater calmness and faster action to less calmness.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-21935-2_23)

## What this does not establish

The abstract omits the full sample and statistical detail. These preliminary associations do not establish treatment effectiveness, lasting anxiety reduction or suitability for every wearer.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-21935-2_23)

## What to look for in the paper

Separate physiological measurements from subjective reports and clinical outcomes. The material interface is a design-research case, not a medical recommendation.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-21935-2_23)
