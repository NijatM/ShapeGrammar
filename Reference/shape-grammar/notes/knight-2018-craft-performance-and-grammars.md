## What it is about

Knight examines craft as performance in time, using traditional Indian kolam pattern making. The focus is on how making unfolds, alongside the design left behind.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-8189-7_16)

## Premise

Can grammars represent temporal actions and the social, aesthetic or creative importance of performance, rather than treating process merely as a way to obtain a product?

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-8189-7_16)

## Method reported

The chapter compares making grammars for craft performance with shape grammars for craft designs through the kolam case. It is a conceptual comparison, not a controlled experiment.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-8189-7_16)

## Reported conclusion

The abstract says the comparison reveals different generative strategies. It does not give enough detail to reconstruct those strategies here.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-8189-7_16)

## Reading boundary

Full-text rules, case selection and supporting discussion remain unreviewed. The abstract does not establish a universal model of every kolam maker’s practice.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-8189-7_16)

## Why follow the original

A final pattern does not record every action that made it. Look for what changes when you describe both material configuration and the maker’s sequence of actions.

[Public abstract](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-8189-7_16)
