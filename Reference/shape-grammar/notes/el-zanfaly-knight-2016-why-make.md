## In brief

El-Zanfaly and Knight propose a guided learning process that connects fabrication skills with independent design and making. [1](https://compmaking.scripts.mit.edu/index/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/El-Zanfaly-Knight-Why-to-make.pdf)

## The question

Technical machine training alone, they argue, can leave students unable to create beyond simple exercises. [1](https://compmaking.scripts.mit.edu/index/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/El-Zanfaly-Knight-Why-to-make.pdf)

## How the work approaches it

Their I3 process moves from imitation, through constrained iteration, to improvisation. Students first make an existing example, then change one aspect at a time, and finally develop their own work. [1](https://compmaking.scripts.mit.edu/index/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/El-Zanfaly-Knight-Why-to-make.pdf)

## What comes out of it

The proposal treats learning to operate tools and learning through material engagement as connected activities. It does not establish measured effectiveness in the inspected passage. [1](https://compmaking.scripts.mit.edu/index/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/El-Zanfaly-Knight-Why-to-make.pdf)
