# Two Exercises in Formal Composition

George Stiny · 1976

Review basis: selected-sections. Primary paper: definitions in §2, both exercises in §3, and conclusions; selected illustrated constructions.

The central framework and both exercises were reviewed. The many illustrated rule variants were sampled rather than exhaustively checked.

## In brief

Two complementary exercises show how to explore families of shapes: start with elements and allowable relations, or start with a drawing and infer rules that generate it and related drawings. ## The question

How can formal composition be investigated systematically while retaining a direct visual connection between rules and the shapes they produce? [1](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf)

## Main idea or claim

Shape grammars make recurring spatial relations explicit. Different choices of vocabulary, relations and decomposition can define different languages, even when they begin with the same visible example. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf)

## How the work approaches it

Exercise 1 chooses polygons, enumerates ways they can share an edge, and turns these relations into recursive rules. Exercise 2 decomposes an existing polygon arrangement, extracts relations, and constructs grammars that also produce further arrangements. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf)

## Evidence and examples

Polygon and triangle constructions illustrate rule variants and finite or infinite generated families. One shape is read as five squares or as two L-shaped components, showing why parsing affects the inferred grammar. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf)

## What comes out of it

A compact rule system can describe both an existing example and a larger family. Recursion supplies constructive explanations of form, rather than only a catalogue of completed drawings. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf)

## Limits to keep in mind

The paper calls the exercises suggestive. Its common-grammar account of style is a modeling assumption, not an empirical test of historical style or proof that functional design requirements are satisfied. [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf)

## What to take from it

Try both directions: generate from relations, then infer alternative relations from a result. Compare the languages each choice permits instead of assuming one decomposition is definitive. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/ramesh/teaching/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny-1976-EPB3_187-210.TwoExerciseInFormalComposition.pdf)
