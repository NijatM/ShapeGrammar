# Weights

George Stiny · 1992

Review basis: selected-sections. Primary scan: shape and label foundations, numerical-weight operations, weighted part relations, and the final coordinated-description example.

Selected definitions and examples were reviewed with OCR and visual checking of p. 416. Other property algebras are not assumed to behave identically to the numerical example.

## In brief

Weights extends shape computation beyond bare geometry. Labels classify elements; weights let properties such as line thickness or tone participate in matching and combination. [1](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf) [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf)

## The question

How should extra properties interact where shapes overlap, and how can several descriptions refer consistently to the same design? [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf)

## Main idea or claim

Properties need algebraic rules of their own. Geometrically identical shapes may behave differently because their labels or weights differ; multiple descriptions can be coordinated through a grammar. [1](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf) [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf)

## How the work approaches it

For numerical weights, the paper uses maximum for sum, minimum for product and subtraction clipped at zero for difference. Geometry is divided into overlap and non-overlap pieces before assigning the appropriate result weights. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf)

## Evidence and examples

Line-thickness and shaded-region examples explain the operations. The concluding cube-row construction coordinates plan, elevation and shaded-area descriptions, using labels and rules to maintain their relationship. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf)

## What comes out of it

Weighted part relations compare both geometry and property values. Coordinated grammars can change one description while requiring corresponding changes in others; geometry alone does not carry all the design information. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf)

## Limits to keep in mind

These weights are formal properties, not physical mass or a universal model of ink blending. Maximum-based addition does not mean arithmetic accumulation. The paper provides constructions rather than measured software or fabrication tests. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf) [3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf)

## What to take from it

Before adding colour or thickness to a shape system, decide how values compare, combine and disappear. Merely drawing a different stroke width leaves the computational meaning unspecified. [2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Stiny.weights.pdf)
