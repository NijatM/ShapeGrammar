## In brief

A formal framework relates design languages by transforming the grammars that generate them.

Evidence: [pp. 129–135: A normal form for shape grammars](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf).

## Question

What should change—and what should remain connected—when one grammar becomes another?

Evidence: [pp. 129–135: A normal form for shape grammars](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf).

## Main idea

A normal form separates spatial composition from control of rule application. Their independent changes can preserve a meaningful structural relationship.

Evidence: [pp. 129–135: A normal form for shape grammars](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf).

## Method

Specify labeled shapes and spatial relations, attach state-label pairs to addition/subtraction rules, then define a recursive dependency relation from selected derivations.

Evidence: [pp. 129–135: A normal form for shape grammars](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf); [pp. 135–141: Recursive structures of shape grammars](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf).

## Worked evidence

One grammar develops into sixteen transformed examples. Directed graphs show the preserved recursive structure; redundant copies of rules permit multiple changed versions of one original rule.

Evidence: [pp. 141–154: Transformations and redundant rules](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf).

## Formal outcome

Shape-change and state-change transformations commute because they act on separate components. Isomorphic recursive structures constrain transformations and transfer understanding of rule dependencies.

Evidence: [pp. 141–154: Transformations and redundant rules](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf).

## Limits

Recursive structure depends on a finite catalogue of chosen derivations. Different understandings of a language can therefore produce different dependency relations; the normal form need not be concise.

Evidence: [pp. 135–141: Recursive structures of shape grammars](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf).

## Why read it

Labels can govern where or how rules apply, while state labels govern when. Grammar transformation must consider both geometry and control.

Evidence: [pp. 129–135: A normal form for shape grammars](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/48-747/subFrames/readings/Knight.transformationOfLanguagesOfDesignsPart2.pdf).

## Terms

- **Recursive structure**: Here, a dependency relation between rules and the initial shape, defined relative to selected derivations.
- **State label**: A nonspatial control indicating which stage or state permits a rule application.
