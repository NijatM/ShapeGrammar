## What the publisher says the book is about

Stiny presents designing as calculation with shapes, emphasizing that ambiguity and changing ways of seeing can support creative work. [1](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262693677/shape/)

## The stated argument

Rule-governed calculation need not be restricted to manipulating numbers or verbal symbols. According to the description, shapes support visual expression whose interpretation can change during design. [1](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262693677/shape/)

## Reported development

The description outlines a progression from seeing versus counting, through formal shape calculation, to specific design examples. Drawings carry much of the exposition, so a text-only synopsis cannot replace reading them. [1](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262693677/shape/)

## What is not established by this review

The publisher’s account identifies the argument; it does not independently validate it. The actual formal definitions, example derivations, implementation requirements and practical outcomes need inspection in the book. [1](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262693677/shape/)

## How to approach the original

Compare what a drawing visibly contains with the particular objects used to describe it. In each illustrated rule application, ask whether a new interpretation becomes possible. Use the formal sections to check the exact permitted operations. ## Key terms

- **Visual calculation**: Applying rules directly to configurations of shapes.
- **Ambiguity**: The possibility of more than one interpretation or division into parts.
