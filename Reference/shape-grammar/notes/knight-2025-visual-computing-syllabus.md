## What this document is

A two-page syllabus for MIT Visual Computing, taught by Knight in Spring 2025. It is a public description of an educational approach and planned sequence, not a research article or a complete set of lessons.

[PDF p. 1: approach and prerequisites](https://architecture.mit.edu/sites/default/files/course-documents/25sp-4.520%2B4.521-syll-knight.pdf)

## Framing idea

The syllabus presents shape grammars as a generative human-intelligence approach grounded in visual and tactile perception. It stresses that computation can be carried out by people and need not begin with programming or a computer.

[PDF p. 1: approach and prerequisites](https://architecture.mit.edu/sites/default/files/course-documents/25sp-4.520%2B4.521-syll-knight.pdf)

## How the exploration is organized

A slow-computing approach uses by-hand and by-eye exercises as the basis for understanding, designing and physical production. Software automation is also introduced. No programming background or programming requirement is stated.

[PDF p. 1: approach and prerequisites](https://architecture.mit.edu/sites/default/files/course-documents/25sp-4.520%2B4.521-syll-knight.pdf)

## The documented progression

The calendar moves from shapes, arithmetic and rules to labelled and parametric rules; then to style analysis and finding rules; then to basic grammars in two and three dimensions, complex/making grammars, and a final project. These are scheduled activities, not evidence that every activity occurred exactly as planned.

[PDF p. 2: calendar](https://architecture.mit.edu/sites/default/files/course-documents/25sp-4.520%2B4.521-syll-knight.pdf)

## What can be concluded

The document establishes how Knight publicly framed this subject and the intended relationship between foundational operations, analysis and making. It provides no measured comparison showing that this teaching method improves learning more than another method.

[PDF p. 1: approach and prerequisites](https://architecture.mit.edu/sites/default/files/course-documents/25sp-4.520%2B4.521-syll-knight.pdf) · [PDF p. 2: calendar](https://architecture.mit.edu/sites/default/files/course-documents/25sp-4.520%2B4.521-syll-knight.pdf)

## A useful personal reading path

Start with manual experiments in seeing and changing shapes, add labels and parameters when they serve a purpose, then try deriving rules from an existing design before inventing a new system. Treat this as a study-sequence suggestion inferred from the calendar, not a substitute for the MIT subject.
