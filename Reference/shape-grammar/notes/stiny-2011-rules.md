# What Rule(s) Should I Use?

George Stiny · 2011

Review basis: selected-sections. Publisher PDF: opening questions, schema lattice and operators, inverse/composite constructions, and concluding art/design discussion.

Selected framework sections were reviewed. The essay’s extensive historical examples were sampled; its studio-education claims are arguments, not reported controlled trials.

## In brief

Instead of prescribing the right rule for a design problem, Stiny offers a small repertoire of ways to make rules. Their power depends on recognizing embedded parts anew when rules are used. [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf)

## The question

How can a designer invent rules that correspond to actions in art and design without needing a fixed vocabulary of components? [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf)

## Main idea or claim

Part selection, transformation and boundary operations can organize rule schemas in a lattice. Identity rules matter too: seeing a shape differently can change its usable parts even when no line moves. [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf)

## How the work approaches it

The essay develops schemas from simple to general, then constructs inverses and combinations. It connects these operations to examples and interpretations from art, geometry and design. [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf) [2](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf)

## Evidence and examples

The support consists of worked visual examples, algebraic relationships and interpretive comparisons. Generic schemas are distinguished from assignments and predicates that give particular stylistic results. [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf) [2](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf) [3](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf)

## What comes out of it

A small set of operations supports a varied rule repertoire, including rules that divide, transform, complete or reinterpret a shape. Hierarchies can be outcomes of these activities instead of prerequisites. [2](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf) [3](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf)

## Limits to keep in mind

The essay explicitly does not solve every design problem or guarantee that a chosen rule meets its goals. Its broad educational proposals and artistic interpretations are not experimental effectiveness results. [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf) [3](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf)

## What to take from it

When stuck, change the kind of operation or the part you see. A schema supplies a way to act; the designer still chooses assignments and judges the resulting design. [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf) [2](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf) [3](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00004-011-0056-6.pdf)
