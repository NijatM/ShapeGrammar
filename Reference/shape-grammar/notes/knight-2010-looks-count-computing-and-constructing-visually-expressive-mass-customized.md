## What it is about

Knight and Sass investigate visually expressive, customizable building assemblies. Shape grammars develop the visual language; digital fabrication develops physical components that can interlock and be assembled by hand.

[Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0890060409990126)

## Premise

Can housing assemblies incorporate culturally meaningful decorative variation while also addressing material and structural requirements? The abstract motivates affordability and local relevance as research goals.

[Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0890060409990126)

## Method reported

This paper reports a first phase of pilot studies for prototype systems using vernacular visual languages. The longer-term ambition is visual–physical grammars that generate complete CAD/CAM information for full-scale fabrication.

[Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0890060409990126)

## Reported outcome

The authors report promising prototypes and multiple strategies for incorporating visual properties in structural systems. The public abstract supplies no shared cost measure, structural test protocol or occupied-housing evaluation.

[Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0890060409990126)

## Claims to keep separate

Low-cost housing and post-disaster applications are intended benefits if the research succeeds, not demonstrated population-level outcomes in this abstract. The individual prototypes require the full paper to assess.

[Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0890060409990126)

## What to look for in the paper

Trace how a decorative rule affects component geometry, assembly and fabrication. Ask which constraints were tested physically and which remain future objectives.

[Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0890060409990126)
