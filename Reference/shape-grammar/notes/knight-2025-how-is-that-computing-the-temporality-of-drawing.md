## What it is about

Knight extends making grammars from describing what happens during drawing to comparing how long alternative sequences might take. A hypothetical grammar for preparatory drawings in Fritz Glarner’s Relational Painting style separates seeing/deciding from drawing/erasing.

[Sections 1–4: process, temporal segmentation and Glarner study](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6)

## Central question

Can a grammar represent duration as well as spatial change? The chapter treats creative making as a process unfolding in time, asking how different ways of reaching a similar final drawing can have different temporal structures.

[Sections 1–4: process, temporal segmentation and Glarner study](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6)

## How the model works

Five stages establish a boundary, subdivide rectangles, add oblique divisions, add wedge borders and finish. Rules alternate sensing and doing states. Gesture time is approximated by stroke length; decision time uses a provisional visual-complexity measure or assigned constants. These are relative model units, not seconds.

[Section 5.1–5.2: timing assumptions; Figs. 7–10](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6) · [Sections 5.3–5.5 and 6: remaining stages, limitations, reflections](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6)

## The worked comparison

Two sequences create the same orthogonal subdivision with reported relative totals of 1490 and 2082. Different intermediate area distributions change the model’s decision-time cost even when the final drawing agrees. Later examples extend the computation through oblique lines and borders.

[Section 5.1–5.2: timing assumptions; Figs. 7–10](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6) · [Sections 5.3–5.5 and 6: remaining stages, limitations, reflections](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6)

## What is demonstrated

The framework can make the order and duration assumptions of a drawing process explicit. Within this model, alternative sequences can differ in decision cost while keeping gesture length fixed. The result is a demonstration of representational possibilities, not a discovery of Glarner’s real working time.

[Section 5.1–5.2: timing assumptions; Figs. 7–10](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6) · [Sections 5.3–5.5 and 6: remaining stages, limitations, reflections](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6)

## Important qualifications

No empirical record of Glarner’s drawing process is available. The complexity measure is a placeholder; constants are chosen for comparison, and backtracking to earlier stages is excluded. The author says real drawing speed could depend on further factors and would require empirical study.

[Sections 1–4: process, temporal segmentation and Glarner study](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6) · [Section 5.1–5.2: timing assumptions; Figs. 7–10](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6) · [Sections 5.3–5.5 and 6: remaining stages, limitations, reflections](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-81623-9_6)

## How to experiment with it

Describe a drawing twice: once as a finished composition, once as a sequence of looking, deciding, marking and revising. Label every timing assumption. Compare sequences under the same assumptions, and keep simulated duration separate from observed duration.
