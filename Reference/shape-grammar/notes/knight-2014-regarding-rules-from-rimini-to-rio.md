## What it is about

Knight asks how studying existing design rules can lead to new work. Alberti’s adaptations of classical architecture introduce a comparison with shape grammars: precedents supply resources for design, while rules can be reused, altered or redirected toward new circumstances.

[pp. 13–18: Alberti, shape grammars and four strategies; Fig. 4](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645)

## Central question

How do we move from analysis to design without merely copying a precedent? The article treats the boundary as flexible: an analytical grammar may already generate new designs, and further transformations can move beyond the original style or change existing buildings directly.

[pp. 13–18: Alberti, shape grammars and four strategies; Fig. 4](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645) · [pp. 18–25: strategies and cases; Figs. 5–9](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645)

## Four distinct strategies

First, use an analysis grammar to generate additional designs within its language. Second, transform that grammar’s rules to generate a changed language. Third, generate or describe existing designs and apply transformation rules to those designs. Fourth, transform existing designs directly without first building an analysis grammar.

[pp. 13–18: Alberti, shape grammars and four strategies; Fig. 4](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645) · [pp. 18–25: strategies and cases; Figs. 5–9](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645)

## How the argument is developed

The strategies are extracted from earlier shape-grammar studies and explained through concise cases. The comparison distinguishes transforming rules from transforming the designs produced by rules. It is a conceptual organization of prior research, not a claim that one strategy always works best.

[pp. 13–18: Alberti, shape grammars and four strategies; Fig. 4](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645) · [pp. 18–25: strategies and cases; Figs. 5–9](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645)

## Examples that clarify the differences

Duarte’s Malagueira grammar illustrates new client-specific designs within an existing style. Knight’s analysis of Vantongerloo illustrates changes between successive rule systems. Colakoglu’s hayat-house transformations adapt traditional plans, while Eloy’s rehabilitation rules modify existing Lisbon housing without an initial generative grammar.

[pp. 18–25: strategies and cases; Figs. 5–9](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645)

## Conclusion and unfinished work

All four strategies can connect precedent with new design intentions; the appropriate choice depends on the problem. The Rocinha investigation is an emerging application: early rules describe building and circulation patterns, but the team still needs a deeper grammar and an account of which socially valuable features to preserve.

[pp. 26–27: Rocinha study and next steps; Fig. 10](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645)

## Boundaries of the evidence

The illustrative grammars are deliberately summarized rather than fully specified here; their original studies are needed to reconstruct or test them. Rocinha’s longer-term design interventions are proposed aims, not completed or evaluated outcomes in this paper.

[pp. 18–25: strategies and cases; Figs. 5–9](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645) · [pp. 26–27: Rocinha study and next steps; Fig. 10](https://impactum-journals.uc.pt/joelho/article/download/_5_1/1645)

## How to use this distinction

Before editing a grammar, decide what should change: its language, its rules or an individual existing design. State which contextual and cultural properties you want to retain. A rule’s ability to generate a novel configuration does not by itself establish that the configuration fits contemporary needs.
