## Short overview

Knight and Stiny extend shape grammars into making grammars: rules describe how people act on materials and sense what they are making. The framework treats making as a computational activity that can remain embodied, improvisational and performed by hand. Drawing and knotting are developed examples; watercolor painting exposes harder, still-open questions.

## Question and premise

Can the perceptual, action-based character of shape computation help describe making with material things? The authors challenge the assumption that designing first supplies a complete plan which passive matter then receives. Their proposed alternative takes the maker’s interactions with materials as central to designing itself.

[Manuscript pp.1–9: §§1–3, perception, action and material algebras](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e41b611c-754f-4539-9961-2e48b16e1318/content#page=2) · [Manuscript pp.17–20: §4.3 and §5, watercolor proposal and discussion](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e41b611c-754f-4539-9961-2e48b16e1318/content#page=18)

## What the theory proposes

A making rule relates sensed things before and after an action or perceptual shift. Its arrow may stand for doing, sensing, or both. The supporting algebra should describe material properties relevant to a maker’s practice, rather than promise a complete account of molecular or mechanical behavior. A computation records chosen moments in a continuous process; it is one interpretation of that process, not the process in its entirety.

[Manuscript pp.1–9: §§1–3, perception, action and material algebras](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e41b611c-754f-4539-9961-2e48b16e1318/content#page=2)

## Method and formal construction

The paper reviews shape replacement, defines making through actions, senses, materials and finite things, and adapts shape algebras to specific materials. It then constructs drawing and knotting examples. In the drawing example, a seeing rule highlights a recognized part without altering the line drawing; a doing rule changes that selected shape. The knotting example separates tactile regrasping from tying and also includes a rule combining both.

[Manuscript pp.1–9: §§1–3, perception, action and material algebras](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e41b611c-754f-4539-9961-2e48b16e1318/content#page=2) · [Manuscript pp.10–11: §4.1 and Fig.3, seeing and drawing](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e41b611c-754f-4539-9961-2e48b16e1318/content#page=11) · [Manuscript pp.11–16: §4.2, Figs.8–9 and string algebra](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e41b611c-754f-4539-9961-2e48b16e1318/content#page=12)

## Worked examples and what they establish

The knotting grammar repeats ties within a knot and knots along a string. Its algebra keeps separate strings separate, even when they overlap visually. A string is described by segments and indexed knots; matching must preserve the correspondence of knots and internal segments. Tying a knot changes that correspondence, so the original unknotted string is not automatically a part of the result. These definitions show why the algebra for fused drawn lines cannot simply be reused for physical strings.

[Manuscript pp.11–16: §4.2, Figs.8–9 and string algebra](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e41b611c-754f-4539-9961-2e48b16e1318/content#page=12)

## Contribution and conclusion

The developed examples make action and sensory attention explicit within rule-based descriptions. The authors conclude that designing can be understood as a form of making involving bodily and perceptual engagement. Their Sargent watercolor discussion instead outlines a research agenda: interacting materials, order-dependent actions, drying and other timed changes may require different operations and combined algebras. It does not supply a finished watercolor model.

## Limits of the evidence

The examples are deliberately schematized. They focus on selected senses and actions, and do not validate a general simulator of knot mechanics, skilled movement or painting. Discrete rule steps leave aspects of continuous performance undescribed. In the watercolor proposal, even familiar algebraic laws and the adequacy of replacement as the action model are questions to investigate.

## What to carry into an experiment

For any rule-based making model, identify what counts as material, thing, action, sensory attention and part. Ask what can change through sensing alone. Then check whether its operations fit that material: adding two overlapping drawn lines and putting two strings together need different accounts. A geometric screen experiment illustrates a selected relationship; it does not reproduce the paper’s tactile practice.

## Useful terms

- **Making grammar:** A rule system describing selected physical changes and sensory interactions with things.
- **Sensing rule:** A rule recording a change of attention or sensory engagement; it need not alter physical geometry.
- **Material-specific part relation:** A definition of containment fitted to the material, including constraints such as corresponding knots.
