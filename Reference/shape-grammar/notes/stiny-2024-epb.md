## What it is about

Stiny reflects on shape grammars’ history in Environment and Planning B and reconsiders the relationship between generating designs and seeing their parts.

[Equations (1)–(3) and discussion](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23998083241246668)

## Central point

Addition rules make compositions; identity rules recognize parts without changing geometry. Stiny now gives analysis and seeing greater prominence than in his early work.

[Equations (1)–(3) and discussion](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23998083241246668)

## How the reflection proceeds

He revisits his two 1976 exercises, connects addition and identity schemas through a summation schema, and describes an embedding-and-fusion cycle.

[Equations (1)–(3) and discussion](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23998083241246668)

## Illustrative evidence

The argument draws on earlier grammatical studies, contributors and personal recollections. It is not an experiment or a new comparative software evaluation.
