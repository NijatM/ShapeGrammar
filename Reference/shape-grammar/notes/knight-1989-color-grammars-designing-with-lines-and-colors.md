## In brief

Color grammars extend shape rules with quality-filled regions, allowing geometry and qualities to be generated together.

Evidence: [pp. 417–420: Representing form and qualities; opening informal discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors).

## Question

What happens when differently colored—or otherwise qualified—regions overlap?

Evidence: [pp. 417–420: Representing form and qualities; opening informal discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors).

## Main idea

A quality field adds information that bare lines cannot carry. Its overlap needs an explicit convention rather than automatic geometric fusion.

Evidence: [pp. 417–420: Representing form and qualities; opening informal discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors); [Color grammars: an informal discussion; rankings and Figures 12–16](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors).

## Method

Add a color-field component to labeled shapes and rules. Rankings determine which coincident qualities dominate or combine, alongside spatial operations.

Evidence: [pp. 417–420: Representing form and qualities; opening informal discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors); [Color grammars: an informal discussion; rankings and Figures 12–16](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors).

## Worked evidence

The same rule sequence in Figures 26 and 27 produces different designs under different rankings: one separates into layers, while the other forms a weave.

Evidence: [pp. 436–439: Color grammar A; Figures 25–27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors).

## Conclusion

Color is one example of a more general quality. Knight explains how an overlap convention could also decide material intersections in an architectural design.

Evidence: [p. 449: Conclusions](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors).

## Limits

These are formal constructions and illustrative interpretations, not empirical tests of painting, materials or buildability. The relation between rankings and apparent layering is not straightforward for every grammar.

Evidence: [p. 449: Conclusions](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors).

## Why read it

Color can participate in computation rather than merely decorate the finished drawing. Geometry alone does not settle how overlapping attributes should behave.

Evidence: [pp. 417–420: Representing form and qualities; opening informal discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors); [p. 449: Conclusions](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23540671_Color_grammars_Designing_with_lines_and_colors).

## Terms

- **Quality field**: A spatial region supplied with qualities such as color; it may be continuous or disconnected.
- **Ranking**: A rule for resolving or combining qualities in coincident regions.
